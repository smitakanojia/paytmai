Business model

A closed clock is the first unit in support you can honestly charge for.

LINE 1 · INTERNAL

Cost and liability avoided

Paytm merchant support. Support cost stays flat through an incident, and penalties, lost chargebacks and Ombudsman cases are avoided. Not revenue — this is what funds the build.

LINE 2 · A Pi SKU

Pay per clock closed

Sold to the banks, lenders and insurers Pi already calls on — the parties who actually owe the penalty. Priced per clock closed, or as a share of liability avoided.

LINE 3 · ATTACH

Revenue at the moment of stress

Rung 4 of the ladder: an instant-settlement advance, already a paid Paytm product, offered at the highest-intent moment there is. Plus retention offers to the merchants an incident put most at risk.

Why the pricing is defensible. Intercom pioneered pay-per-resolution, but “resolved” is unverifiable — it can mean the customer went quiet. A closed clock has a timestamp, a rupee value and a regulator’s definition. You can invoice it without arguing about it.

Why it compounds. Every circular encoded is reusable across every bank. The audit trail lives here, so leaving means losing your regulatory evidence. A foreign vendor would need an India payments team to begin.

The strongest idea on this slide is the pricing unit. Support has never had an honestly billable outcome, because resolution is self-reported — that is exactly the Intercom critique from our problem slide, now turned into a business advantage. A closed clock is defined by a regulator, carries a timestamp and has a rupee value attached, so outcome pricing finally works. If you only have time for one sentence here, use that one.
