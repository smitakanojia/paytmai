Proposed solution · holding it at scale

When a bank drops, five thousand clocks start in the same minute.

What already exists — we are not claiming it

Incident correlation is twenty years old. Zendesk links child incidents to a problem ticket and detects recurring patterns. ServiceNow runs Major Incident Management with similar-incident prediction.

Playbook induction exists too: Decagon’s Duet Autopilot drafts procedures from transcripts for human approval.

What does not exist

Nobody has wired the agent to the incident. Zendesk’s own documentation states that AI agent tickets cannot be linked to problem and incident tickets.

So the ITSM layer sees one outage while the AI layer re-diagnoses it five thousand times — and no layer at all is counting down to T+1.

Our batching: one diagnosis, five thousand clocks stopped before T+1, one ops approval, each merchant told their own amount and time.

Said honestly: technical declines ran 0.02% in Feb 2025. Outages are our stress case, not our business case — the clock engine earns its keep every ordinary day.

Concede first. Every team in this room will claim clustering is novel; it is not, and a judge can check in thirty seconds. What we claim is narrower and verifiable: Zendesk's own docs say their AI agent tickets cannot link to incidents. Then the honesty line about the 0.02 percent decline rate — this pre-empts the obvious attack that we built a product for a rare day, and it is why the clock engine, not the outage, is the product.
