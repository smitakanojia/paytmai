Why it must be autonomous · Track 3

Autonomy here is not a cost saving. It is the only thing that meets the deadline.

A T+1 clock cannot queue

One deadline is a task for a person. Thousands of simultaneous deadlines, each with its own amount and its own expiry hour, is not work a human queue can hold — no matter how it is staffed.

Nobody opens the ticket

A merchant cannot ask for compensation they have never heard of. If the agent waits to be asked, the money is never paid and the breach is never recorded.

The window is a cycle, not a day

A chargeback is decided on what is raised in the next settlement cycle. Silence does not postpone the case — it loses it.

A human is more accurate than an agent. A human is not faster than a clock.

This is the slide that answers the track question — why an autonomous teammate rather than a better assistant. Most teams will argue autonomy because it is cheaper, and that argument loses to a judge who points out a human is more accurate. We are not arguing cost. We are arguing that the deadline is non-negotiable and arrives in volume. Deliver the last line plainly, without emphasis.
